₹1 Crore ka Mathematics

Revenue. Gross profit. Net profit. Teen bilkul alag numbers.

Title ek mathematics point hai, promise nahi. Book is target ko exactly kaise todti hai, woh yahan dekhiye — ₹999 ke order ke usi worked example ke saath.

₹27,397 roz ka revenue
₹1 crore ÷ 365
~27 ₹999 par orders/day
₹1 crore revenue ke liye
~160 ₹171 contribution par orders/day
₹1 crore contribution ke liye

₹999 ka order actually kahan jaata hai EXAMPLE

Selling price (customer deta hai)₹999
GST component (5% inclusive, illustrative)− ₹48
Manufacturing cost− ₹400
Packaging− ₹20
Gross profit₹531
Marketplace commission + fees (illustrative)− ₹200
Shipping (illustrative)− ₹60
Advertising cost per order− ₹100
Net contribution per delivered order₹171

Part 14 se. Fees, shipping aur GST assumptions hain — inhe apne actual numbers se replace karein. Overheads, returns aur taxes iske baad aate hain.

₹1 crore revenue

Customers se aaya total order value.

₹1,00,00,000 ÷ ₹999 ≈ 10,010 orders/year ≈ 27/day

₹1 crore gross profit

Taxable value minus product aur packaging cost.

₹1,00,00,000 ÷ ₹531 ≈ 18,832 orders/year ≈ 52/day

₹1 crore net profit

Har cost, return, overhead aur tax ke baad.

Iske liye kahin zyada orders chahiye — aapke overheads aur returns par depend karta hai. Book iska calculation karna sikhati hai.

Margin kyon matter karta hai EXAMPLE

₹100 contribution/order~274/day
₹171 contribution/order~160/day
₹250 contribution/order~110/day

₹1 crore/year ke liye roz kitne orders chahiye. Margin better ho to orders ki zaroorat kam hoti hai.

₹1 crore ek mathematical business target hai — guaranteed income ya result nahi. Upar diye gaye saare numbers ebook ke worked examples hain, jinki assumptions saaf likhi hain. Aapke costs, fees, returns aur results alag honge. Book khud kehti hai ki yeh target tabhi possible hai jab product sahi ho, cost control ho, returns kam hon aur testing lagatar chalti rahe — aur yeh har kisi ke liye easy ya fast nahi hai.
